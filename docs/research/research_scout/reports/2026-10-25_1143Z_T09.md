### 1. Sobti, "Deflate by Bits, Not Trials": trial-count DSR under-penalises adaptive (human or AI-assisted) search; sealed PASS/FAIL holdout as remedy
- **Primary URL:** https://github.com/shloksobti/deflate-by-bits (paper: SSRN 7557458)
- **Date and type:** 2026 (no exact date shown; 4 commits); preprint plus MIT code. Reading: main scout read the repo README; a sub-scout read the paper.
- **Summary:** The DSR assumes the trials were fixed in advance and only counted. When each new trial is built from what worked before ("combine the winners"), the best validation t-statistic on pure noise is CLAIMED to grow like sqrt(2k/pi), far faster than the DSR bar of about sqrt(2 ln k), so a ledger-based DSR certifies noise. The paper proposes charging for the information the holdout reveals instead: a sealed holdout that only answers PASS/FAIL, with a fixed query budget K, costs about log K.
- **Claimed effect / data:** synthetic noise; an LLM agent told to maximise validation Sharpe reached t up to 15.7 on noise and passed a ledger DSR under optional stopping; an RL miner learned the same ensemble; real-data check on 423 hedged Ken French industry strategies with a stationary-bootstrap null. Not crypto.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:** none found; single-author, unreviewed, AI-assisted (author says so). The sqrt(2k/pi) rate is for a specific adaptive ensemble, not all search.
- **Risks:** the remedy only works if the holdout really is sealed (repo-level discipline, not code); log K accounting still needs K fixed in advance.
- **Data, cost, licence:** no market data needed to adopt the protocol; free; MIT.
- **Relation to repo:** CryptKeep research is largely iterated with AI assistants across many sessions, so the N in `dsr_btc_eth_trend_20261011/README.md` (judged 10-15) and N = 28 in `ctrend_oos_20261022/PREREGISTRATION_DRAFT.md` line 175 are trial counts of exactly the kind this paper says can be too small. No repo hit for "adaptive search" or "sealed".
- **Bounded next step (operator approval):** a written-only proposal for a sealed-holdout rule (who holds the holdout, PASS/FAIL-only answers, query budget K fixed in the pre-registration). No data touched.
- **Status:** UNTESTED.

### 3. Power and minimum detectable effect for Sharpe comparisons: Gao, "Underpowered by Construction", with López de Prado, Lipton & Zoonekynd, "How to Use the Sharpe Ratio" (PSR/MinTRL/power)
- **Primary URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7486559 (also https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5520741)
- **Date and type:** Sep 2026, independent SSRN working paper (16 pages); about Oct 2025 working paper related to JPM 52(6). Reading: abstracts / summary page only. The main scout's title search did not return the Gao paper, so its existence rests on the sub-scout's SSRN read.
- **Summary:** Gao applies the Jobson-Korkie test with Memmel's correction and shows that the sample needed to detect a Sharpe difference is driven by effect size and by the correlation with the benchmark. At correlation 0.76-0.95, detecting a 0.10 Sharpe difference at 80% power is CLAIMED to need 128-610 years of monthly data; minimum detectable effects in the study were 0.18-0.36. Recommends always reporting the correlation and the MDE. López de Prado et al. list five Sharpe-inference errors (normality, sample size, power, p-value as probability strategy is real, multiple testing) and update PSR, MinTRL and DSR.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:** Gao uses normal-theory Jobson-Korkie on macro/60-40 data; López de Prado et al. grade their own methods.
- **Risks:** crypto vol and heavy tails make power worse; MDE for short new-coin histories may be implausibly large, which is itself the finding.
- **Data, cost:** none beyond saved return series; free.
- **Relation to repo:** `ctrend_oos_20261022/PREREGISTRATION_DRAFT.md` line 178 already states power from tracking error; `information_edge_assessment_20261018.md` line 72 asks for an MDE. No repo hit for MinTRL, Jobson or "track record".

- **Status:** UNTESTED.

- **Summary:** Compares estimators of the true signal-to-noise ratio of the in-sample winner: naive maximum, grand mean, James-Stein, GMLEB, SURE, empirical Bayes, polyhedral-lemma estimators and a Bailey et al. expected-max correction. ### 7. Backtest-to-live decay prior: Liu, "Evaluating Structured Strategy Backtests" (1,726 bank strategies), with Zhu & Cai's AI-trading survey

- **Summary:** Across 1,726 bank-distributed rules-based strategies (2009-2025), vol-targeted mean return CLAIMED to fall from 3.6% to 1.5% a year over six months live and 4.1% to 1.0% over twelve (both p<0.01); the backtest predicts live returns with coefficient 0.137; a leave-one-out peer-bucket benchmark absorbs about 75% of it; live drawdown worse for 54%. - **Relation to repo:** no hit for haircut or backtest-to-live; peer-bucket benchmark resembles comparing a candidate to its own parameter family.
- **Bounded next step:** operator decides whether paper-trading reviews should record a stated haircut prior next to the backtest figure. Text only.
