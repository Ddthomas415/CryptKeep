### 1. Cross-Sectional Dispersion and the State Dependence of Cryptocurrency Momentum (Zhang & Makgolo)
- **Primary URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6648082
- **Date and type:** SSRN, posted 2026-04-25, revised 2026-04-27. Preprint. Abstract only; it was blocked in the first T04 pass and loaded this time.
- **Summary:** The universe is a dynamic CoinGecko top-500, with rolling screens on age, market cap, trading activity and return quality, built to avoid survivorship.
  - The authors claim momentum stays economically meaningful.
  - Momentum breakdowns are predicted by lagged cross-sectional return dispersion, not by BTC volatility or average correlation. The relation is nonlinear and concentrated in high-dispersion states.
  - Scaling by dispersion improves full-sample drawdowns and certainty-equivalent returns.
  - In **post-2020 long-only** implementations, scaling by BTC volatility works better.
  - The conclusions depend on how the universe is built.
- **Claimed effect:** state-dependent momentum; a BTC-volatility scale helps the long-only leg after 2020. The abstract gives no cost or turnover figures.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:**
  - Borri et al. (SEEN T04-2), Brigida (2023-24, momentum not priced) and Grobys et al. (SEEN T04-4) find momentum weak or absent post-2020.
  - The repo's E6 FAIL.
  - Lead 5's live products lost money in 2025-26.
- **Risks:** the screen choices ("return quality") are a forking-paths surface; the authors say results depend on them. Abstract-level reading only.
- **Data, cost, licence:** CoinGecko history; the free Demo plan covers only 365 days per the sub-scout's docs read, so full history is paid. Coinbase candles in the repo archive could compute dispersion over the Coinbase universe.
- **Repo relation:** the CTREND-OOS draft holds the top quintile unscaled.
  - A state variable (dispersion or BTC vol) would be a **separate, later** hypothesis, adding to the CTREND and E6 trial family.
  - Vol scaling already exists in the BTC/ETH candidate (`composite_v1_20261013/PREREGISTRATION.md` line 13).
- **Next step (needs operator approval):** read the full paper and record the post-2020 long-only, after-cost table, if one exists. Do not add a scaling arm to CTREND-OOS before it freezes. Any overlay would be its own pre-registration after CTREND-OOS reports.
- **Status:** UNTESTED

### 2. Market Efficiency in a Changing Asset Population: Mobility, Entry, and Survival over Cryptocurrency's Full History (Ballis & Ioannidis)
- **Primary URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23283v2
- **Date and type:** arXiv, Oct 2026 (v2). Preprint. Full text read to about 100k characters; the last ~12k characters were not read.
- **Summary:** CoinMarketCap weekly data, Apr 2013 to Jul 2026, 8,970 assets, split into frontier (2013-17), expansion (2017-21) and consolidation (2021-26) eras.
  - **Size:** on a tradable universe in the consolidation era, the size gradient is 0.01%/week (t = 0.04).
    - A $10k daily-volume screen removes the frontier-era premium (t = 0.19).
    - A one-cent minimum price cuts it from 27.6% to 9.4% a week.
    - Counting delistings as total losses cuts it by about a quarter.
    - Weekly turnover is 17-47% and median capacity is $293k.
  - **Entry and exit:** weekly entry into the universe fell from 3.93% to 0.75%, and exit from 1.85% to 0.56%. The exit hazard is about 2.0%/week in the bottom size decile and 0.2% in the top decile.
- **Claimed effect:** the size/neglect premium is not tradable after 2021. Exit hazard is strongly size-graded.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:**
  - CF Benchmarks Size factor +22.81% YTD to Oct 15, 2026 (lead 5). That is gross, long-short, top-50 and outlier-driven.
  - Brigida finds size priced in 2023-24 but not under a $500M screen.
- **Risks:** unrefereed. CMC volumes may be inflated. The unread tail may hold caveats. It does not test momentum, CTREND or top-100-entrant returns.
- **Data, cost, licence:** none needed to use it as evidence. Its entry/exit hazards could serve as an external sanity check on the LOW_VALUE_TOP100 and ENTR-CB-1 dead-coin counts.
- **Repo relation:** consistent with survey line 25 (size dies under constraints). It is the first post-2021 tradable-universe estimate the scout has found. It supports avoidance of size tilts.
- **Next step (needs operator approval):** add it as a dated citation to the survey's cross-sectional paragraph. Compare its top-decile exit hazard (about 0.2%/week) with the repo's own top-100 exit counts in `low_value_top100_20261018/by_asset_v2.csv`, as a desk check only, with no new data.

### 6. Cryptocurrency Momentum: Is It an Illusion? (Grobys & Shahzad)
- **Primary URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/ijfiec/v31y2026i2p2180-2193.html (SSRN 4633099; DOI 10.1002/ijfe.70036)
- **Date and type:** International Journal of Finance & Economics 31(2), Apr 2026. Peer-reviewed. Abstract only.
  - Listed as blocked in the first T04 pass, not as a lead. What is new: it is now published, and its abstract was read via IDEAS.
- **Summary:** The realized variances of six crypto momentum strategies follow power laws. Bootstrap tests suggest the mean and variance may be undefined, so Sharpe ratios and t-statistics may not exist, and the premium may not be harvestable.
- **Claimed effect:** inference on crypto momentum spreads is unreliable because of infinite-variance tails.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:** CTREND reports t = 5.19 on a long-short spread, and Borri et al. (SEEN) report t = 3.7 post-2020. Those standard t-stats are exactly what this paper questions. Its power-law estimation on short samples is itself fragile.
- **Risks:** abstract only; the strategy specs are unknown.
- **Data, cost, licence:** none.
- **Repo relation:** CTREND-OOS (draft r2) bases PASS/FAIL on mean excess returns. This lead argues for also pre-stating tail and drawdown reporting, and possibly a heavy-tail-robust test, for the top quintile. The draft's integrity perturbations (section 12) are related but not the same.
- **Next step (needs operator approval):** have the second independent reviewer of CTREND-OOS consider whether a tail-robust check (e.g. a median or trimmed-mean sign test, reported only) belongs in r3.
- **Status:** UNTESTED
