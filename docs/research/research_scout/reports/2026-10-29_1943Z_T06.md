## Leads (ranked by plausible after-cost value to CryptKeep x feasibility with free or cheap data)

### 1. Google Trends abnormal search as a weekly predictor for 40 top-100 coins (Guidolin & Ionta, JIMF 2026)
- **URL:** https://iris.unibocconi.it/handle/11565/4081736 (full text PDF linked from the record; SSRN 5079335). **Date:** 2026 (JIMF vol. 107, art. 102285). **Type:** peer-reviewed.
- **Summary:** The authors build three weekly factor-mimicking portfolios (active addresses, hashrate, and Google Trends abnormal search measured as deviation from the prior 4-week average, following Liu & Tsyvinski) and use them to forecast weekly returns for 40 coins drawn from the top 100 by volume (13 PoW, 27 non-mineable). They then sort coins by realised out-of-sample predictability and go long the most predictable, short the least, rebalanced monthly.
- **Claimed effect:** all three predictors give positive OOS certainty-equivalent gains; long-short spreads described as "large" with significant alphas. Magnitudes not seen (Table 6 truncated in what was read). Data: Coin Metrics prices, about 2018 to April 2025.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:** the authors note search volume is partly driven by prices (their footnote 10); Kang et al. (lead 2) find prices drive sentiment rather than the reverse; survey line 37 says sentiment mostly repackages momentum. U.Today (2026-10-15) reports BTC and XRP Google search interest at multi-year lows, which shows the series is regime-dependent; no return statistics there.
- **Risks:** OOS window stated inconsistently (2020-2025 in text vs 2018-2025 in a table); universe filtered on data availability (survivorship; some series end early); no transaction costs mentioned; the headline strategy is long-short, which a Coinbase spot account cannot hold; sorting on realised predictability adds model selection. Google Trends values are sampled and rescaled per request, so stored history is not exactly reproducible.
- **Data/cost/licence:** Google Trends (free, rate-limited, Google ToS); Coin Metrics community tier (free, limited); hashrate only for PoW coins.
- **Repo relation:** no prior repo test; ATTN-1 used Wikipedia, not Google Trends. Hoang & Vo (SEEN, T06) is the closest earlier row.
- **Next step (needs operator approval):** read Table 6 and the long-leg-only results; if the long-only predictable-coin leg is reported net of plausible costs, draft a pre-registration for a long-only weekly Google-Trends-abnormal-search filter on the existing low-value top-100 universe with momentum as the control.
- **Status:** UNTESTED.

### 2. Counter-evidence: global news sentiment follows prices, not the reverse (Kang, Hwang, Shin & Yoo, EJF 2026)
- **URL:** https://scholarx.skku.edu/item/06dca7e9-9021-4c5a-bc73-ae0f71a48bd8. **Date:** April 2026 (European Journal of Finance 32(8), 1006-1036). **Type:** peer-reviewed.
- **Summary:** About 500k news articles (Dec 2017 to Aug 2023) for Korean and global markets were scored with ChatGPT. In the global market past price changes predict news sentiment, not the other way round. Only Korean news sentiment predicts returns, and that disappears when KRW volume exceeds USD volume, because arbitrage then synchronises prices.
- **Claimed effect:** no predictive value of global-market news sentiment for a USD trader.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:** none found contradicting it for the global market this run; Guidolin & Ionta (lead 1) use search, not news.
- **Risks:** ChatGPT training data overlaps the sample (look-ahead in the scorer); mostly BTC and large coins; abstract-only read.
- **Data/cost:** news archive plus LLM API; not needed unless the operator wants to replicate the null.
- **Repo relation:** supports the information-edge assessment's choice to deprioritise news (`information_edge_assessment_20261018.md`) and the news-ingestion candidate staying unwired.
- **Next step (needs operator approval):** record as the default prior against any LLM news-sentiment candidate; require any future news-sentiment pre-registration to test price-to-sentiment Granger direction first.
- **Status:** UNTESTED.

### 4. GitHub forks, watches, issues and pull requests vs crypto prices (Petryk, Qiu & Pathak, JMIS 2023)
- **URL:** https://jmis-web.org/articles/1645. **Date:** 2023 (JMIS 40(4), 1237-1270). **Type:** peer-reviewed.
- **Summary:** Treats each coin as both a software project and an asset and links GitHub community signals to prices. Claims forks associate with about +4.3% annual price, watches +2.4%, issues +4.4%, pull requests about -5%.
- **Claimed effect:** annual-horizon associations, not a timing signal. Period and sample not shown in the abstract.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:** effects are small relative to crypto volatility; CFR WP 25-02 (lead 8) finds project-account activity predicts code innovation, not returns; industry reports (CoinDesk 2026-03-12, secondary) say crypto GitHub commits fell sharply in 2025-26, so the regime may differ.
- **Risks:** repo-to-coin mapping, star/fork gaming, survivorship (coins with active repos), likely in-sample panel regressions.
- **Data/cost:** GitHub API (free, rate-limited) or Electric Capital Open Dev Data (SEEN, T06).
- **Repo relation:** no repo work; CoinGecko dev data removal is a SEEN T06 row.
- **Next step (needs operator approval):** read the full paper for sample, period and whether any out-of-sample portfolio was formed; only then consider a slow (quarterly) filter pre-registration.
- **Status:** UNTESTED.
