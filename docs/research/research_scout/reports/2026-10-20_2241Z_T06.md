### 1. Social-attention spikes before reversals in small coins (Ardia & Bluteau; Moser & Brauneis; Qureshi & Zaman)
- **Primary URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.02148v1 (also https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v54y2023ics1544612323001551.html and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.02911)
- **Date and type:** Ardia & Bluteau, "The Role of Twitter in Cryptocurrency Pump-and-Dumps", preprint June 2023 (Maître et al. cite it as 2024; venue not verified); full text read via fetch summary. Moser & Brauneis, "Should you listen to crypto YouTubers?", Finance Research Letters 54 (2023), peer-reviewed, abstract only. Qureshi & Zaman, "Social Media Engagement and Cryptocurrency Performance", PLOS ONE 2023, peer-reviewed; arXiv preprint read (see header disclosure).
- **Summary:** Ardia and Bluteau match 322 "successful" Telegram-organised pump-and-dumps (93 coins, Binance BTC pairs, Feb 2019 to Feb 2022) with tweets around each event. Abnormal tweet volume rises before the pump and goes with larger losses after the dump: Twitter-reliant buyers appear to sell late. Moser and Brauneis find that low-cap coins covered in 305 YouTube videos show positive abnormal returns and volume that reverse shortly afterwards. Qureshi and Zaman study 48 tokens launched 2019-2021 via ICO/IEO/IDO: very low and very high first-month engagement both go with low later returns, and higher bot probability goes with lower returns. Read together, these suggest a long-only **avoidance** rule: do not buy a lower-value coin in the days after a social-attention spike. An avoidance filter removes trades rather than adding them, so it adds no fee drag.
- **Claimed effect:** Ardia & Bluteau Table 3: +0.325% pre-event CAR per 1 SD of abnormal tweets; post-dump CAR (hours 32-720) -2.202% (simple model) and -1.256% (with controls) per SD. The text quotes -2.202% as the controlled estimate, which conflicts with the table. Moser & Brauneis: no magnitude in the abstract. Qureshi & Zaman: top-quartile engagement one-month returns "near 200% in some periods" (bull-market sample); one-year returns negative for 2021 entries.
- **Strongest counter-evidence:** Maître et al. (lead 3) find no reversal after organic Twitter attention, and Haase et al. (lead 5) find crowd buy signals predict positive short-term moves in small coins. The sign may depend on event type (organised pump vs organic attention). All samples end by 2022; all results are in-sample; none models costs.
- **Risks:** event selection ("successful" pumps only, from Telegram scraping); in-sample regressions; Botometer error; bull-market sample; survivorship in token lists; hour-scale event timing does not map cleanly to daily Coinbase data.
- **Data, cost, licence:** X data is now paid (pay-per-use reads CLAIMED at $0.005 per post, Medianama 2026-02-07). Free proxies: Wikipedia pageviews and Google Trends API (lead 2) and the repo's existing CMC trending log.
- **Repo relation:** survey ranks avoidance filters as robust negative screens (table row 3) and excludes pump participation (line 31); CMC trending log is the repo's existing attention series (`edge_map_20261013.md` line 60). No repo hit for Ardia, Bluteau, Moser, Qureshi or Zaman.
- **Next step (needs operator approval):** draft, but do not run, a forward-only avoidance pre-registration: universe = Coinbase-tradable top-100 excluding the top 20; spike = a free attention series (Wikipedia user-agent pageviews or CMC trending appearance) above a pre-set multiple of its own 30-day median; outcome = 7- and 30-day return vs BTC for flagged vs unflagged coin-days; pass rule, minimum count and trial-ledger entry fixed before any data are viewed.
- **Status:** UNTESTED

### 2. Free, reproducible attention series: Wikimedia per-article pageviews and the Google Trends API (alpha)

- **Date and type:** Wikimedia Analytics API docs (current); Google Trends API alpha announced 2025-07-24 (news coverage dated 2025-07-28). Docs.
- **Summary:** Wikimedia's API has served per-article pageviews since its 2015-11-01 release, as a daily series (view data start July 2015 per ElBahrawy et al.). An "automated" agent type for heuristically detected bots was added on 2020-04-29, alongside the older user-agent-based "spider" type, so "user" counts before and after April 2020 may not be comparable. ### 3. Maître, Pugachyov, Weigert: "Social media-based attention and the cross-section of cryptocurrency returns"
- **Primary URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v178y2025ics0378426625001384.html (working paper read: https://cfr-cologne.de/download/workingpaper/cfr-25-02.pdf)
- **Date and type:** Journal of Banking & Finance 178 (2025), peer-reviewed. CFR Working Paper 25-02 (2024-11-05) read via fetch summary; the journal version was not read.
- **Summary:** Abnormal daily tweet counts per coin (log tweets today minus log of the prior 30-day mean) for about 165 of the largest coins as of end-2017, 2018-2022, from the Twitter academic API; prices from CoinMarketCap via crypto2 with survivorship correction. User "$ticker" tweets drive the effect. Official project tweets do not predict returns but do predict later GitHub commits. Next-day predictability is confined to the below-median (small-cap) half, which the authors attribute to limits to arbitrage. Regressions only: the WP reports no portfolio sorts, no long-only leg and no horizon beyond t+3 days.
- **Claimed effect:** WP Tables 7-8: abnormal attention coefficient 1.06 same day and 0.16 next day (ticker tweets 1.11 / 0.17). Per 1 cross-sectional SD: about +0.70-0.72% same day and +0.11% next day (authors annualise to about 39%). Table 11: large caps next day 0.03 (t=0.68, not significant); small caps 0.13 (t=2.89). The size-split caption and column labels are inconsistent in the WP.

A gross +0.11% next-day effect is far below the 0.50-1.00% Coinbase round-trip hurdle (survey line 3) unless the signal persists over several days, which the WP did not test. Söylemezgil (2023) finds no explanatory power among the top 10 coins.
- **Risks:** only the top 100 tweets per day were collected (Twitter relevance ranking undisclosed); deleted accounts; ticker homonyms; renamed coins excluded (look-ahead the authors call small); sample ends 2022.

On 2026-01-15 X revoked API access from apps that reward posting (The Block), and Kaito closed its Yaps leaderboards: social-data access can change abruptly. CoinGecko removed `twitter_followers` on 2025-05-15, citing X API changes.
- **Repo relation:** newer peer-reviewed support for the attention effect the survey calls decayed (lines 3, 37), but only for small caps, next-day and gross. No repo hit.

### 8. Look-ahead bias in LLM-scored news sentiment (Glasserman & Lin)
- **Primary URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322
- **Date and type:** arXiv, 2023-09-29. Preprint. Abstract only.
- **Summary:** When an LLM's training data overlaps the backtest period, the model may already "know" what followed a headline. The authors compare original and company-anonymised headlines inside and after the model's training window. Inside the window, anonymised headlines did better, which they read as a "distraction" effect larger than look-ahead; after the cutoff, look-ahead was not a concern.
